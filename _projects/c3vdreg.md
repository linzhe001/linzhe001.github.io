**Local-to-Local Colonoscopic Point-Cloud Registration Benchmark · arXiv:2511.00260**

Linzhe Jiang, Jiayuan Huang, Sophia Bano, Matthew J. Clarkson, Zhehua Mao, Mobarak I. Hoque

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00260) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00260) · [Code](https://github.com/linzhe001/C3VDReg)

## Overview

Anatomy-aware colonoscopic navigation requires localizing each partial endoscopic observation on a stable 3D reference — for coverage assessment, revisited-region awareness, and, in the longer term, linking intraoperative video with pre-operative CT-derived anatomy. Yet rigid point-cloud registration in the colon differs fundamentally from standard indoor or outdoor benchmarks: the surface is locally homogeneous, haustral folds repeat, observations are highly partial, and reconstructed depth is noisy.

C3VDReg is a dataset and benchmark derived from the public Colonoscopy 3D Video Dataset (C3VD), which was captured using a rigid silicone colon phantom. For each frame, a source point cloud is generated by reprojecting the released depth map, and a target point cloud by raycasting the reference mesh from the matched camera pose. The benchmark inherits camera poses from C3VD's 2D-3D registration pipeline; its reported 0.321 mm and 0.159 degree validation errors were measured in simulation rather than on the real phantom videos.

## Highlights

- **10,015** viewpoint-matched partial-to-partial point-cloud pairs, including **2,088** held-out test pairs across six held-out sequences, under a fixed protocol: 8,192 points per cloud, source-only perturbations, and a single pose convention.
- Benchmarks public baselines (GeoTransformer, REGTR, DCP, PointNetLK-style methods) under one shared contract.
- Despite **74.3–93.1%** ground-truth overlap between source and target, registration recall remains low across all evaluated methods.
- Under this fixed protocol, error decomposition suggests **translation ambiguity along repetitive tubular anatomy** — sliding along the lumen — as a major bottleneck. A perturbation-magnitude ablation is still needed to separate intrinsic anatomical ambiguity from initialization difficulty.
